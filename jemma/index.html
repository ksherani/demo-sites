The beginning

Understand & prepare

We listen carefully, assess your case and develop a tailored plan.

  • Case assessment: review the situation, key issues and potential strategies.
  • Client onboarding: help you feel comfortable and informed.
  • Strategic planning: outline the steps towards your desired outcome.
  • Legal guidance: explain rights, obligations and available options.

Along the way

Guide & support

Consistent support as your case develops, right up to the final hearing.

  • Regular updates: keep you informed about progress and new developments.
  • Advocacy and representation support: discuss the assistance available for your matter and forum.

Facing court on your own?

Preparation brings
confidence.

When approaching a case as a litigant in person, focus on facts and evidence. Courts work through established procedures. An objective, organised approach helps you navigate your case.

Find support for your case
Understand the rules

Familiarise yourself with the family court rules and welfare checklist, and how the judge will refer to them.

Get familiar with the court

Where appropriate, visiting a court can help you understand the atmosphere and feel less nervous about the process.

Keep track of every deadline

Follow court directions and deadlines carefully. Missing them can have serious consequences for your case.

Look for affordable guidance

You do not have to manage everything alone. Seek guidance to understand your position and explore available free consultations.

Seek practical support

Support Through Court can assist with court attendance and basic legal documents. Jemma has previously worked there and recommends it to people facing court alone.

Communicate clearly

Keep communications concise, factual and polite. Avoid inflammatory language when responding to the other party.

Listen carefully to the judge

Judges cannot act as your lawyer, but they give practical directions. Pay attention and respond promptly to their recommendations.
